Research focus

Creativity in music education

My PhD produced an original theoretical framework and model of creativity in music education. It draws on Csikszentmihalyi's Domain-Field-Individual model, among other theories. His "Field" is the gatekeeper that decides which new ideas get passed on. The model explains how teachers handle three creativity dimensions set by the curriculum: sound exploration, improvisation and composition. A related peer-reviewed article is forthcoming in Music Education Research.

A project of our own

Syn-Aesthetica — 2025–present

Dong Ding and I started this in early 2025, soon after we both finished our PhDs. No funding. No brief. Just our own read on where music, AI and physical interfaces are heading, and a lot of curiosity.

The idea: read the music (its audio features, its emotional tone) and let a sculpture move to it. Ding (jewellery-design PhD, Edinburgh College of Art) designs the mechanisms. I build the analysis pipeline, train a lightweight emotion classifier on public affective-music datasets, and work out how you would evaluate whether it teaches anyone anything. The tool is Python/C++ and still a prototype.

We're sharing it early because we're both at the start of our careers, and we want to talk with people in the field. Two papers are in the works: a scoping review of the field (Ding leads; aimed at CoDesign) and a software paper (I lead; aimed at JOSS).

Education

PhD in Education, University of Glasgow · 01/2019–06/2024

Thesis: Music teachers' perceptions of creativity in the context of twenty-first century Chinese education (viva 27 September 2023). Supervisors: Prof. Oscar Odena, Prof. Kay Livingston.

Qualitative design: 17 semi-structured interviews, 24 lesson observations, critical-incident charting, researcher diary; thematic analysis in NVivo. Produced an original seven-stage model of creativity development in music education. Key finding: conservatoire-trained instrumentalists received formal improvisation training; generalist teachers engaged with it only informally.
